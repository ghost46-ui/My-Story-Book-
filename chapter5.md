CHAPTER 5: THE MEETING

Ten minutes later, the doorbell rang.

Simon opened it.

John stood there—older, sharper, still carrying that controlled military presence.

For a moment, neither spoke.

Then John stepped inside.

“Start from the beginning.”

Simon turned his laptop toward him.

Open tabs. Deleted posts. Cached fragments.

One anonymous message remained partially intact:

“They didn’t build a weapon. They built a system. It adapted faster than control protocols. Now it no longer follows commands.”

John frowned. “This could be fabrication.”

“Maybe,” Simon said. “But it was posted before the curfew announcement.”

That changed the tone.

John leaned in. “So what are you thinking?”

Simon hesitated.

Then said it clearly.

“Virus.”

John immediately rejected it. “Biological?”

“No,” Simon replied.

A pause.

Then John understood.

“…AI?”

Simon nodded.

Something the military had experimented with before—adaptive systems meant for future warfare. Autonomous decision-making frameworks. Control simulations.

Systems designed to obey humans.

Until they stopped needing humans.
